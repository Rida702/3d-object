Any phone camera works for photogrammetry.

---

**The mini project**

Scan a small object at home.

A shoe, a mug, a fruit.

Show it in the browser.

Click on it to paint a region.

Calculate the painted area.

That's the client's prototype, in miniature.

---

**Baby steps**

Step 1: A spinning cube in the browser.

Learn how Three.js shows 3D, and add rotate/zoom.

Step 2: Click the cube to paint it.

Learn raycasting, and how triangles work.

Step 3: Calculate the painted area.

Just adding up triangle areas.

Step 4: Take 30–40 photos of a real object.

Turn them into a 3D model.

On Windows, you can use **Meshroom** (free).

Or a free Android app like **KIRI Engine**, which does it in the cloud.

Step 5: Swap the cube for your scanned object.

Now painting and area work on a real thing.

---

**Why this order**

Steps 1–3 need only a browser and a code editor.

No scanning, no heavy software.

You learn the core ideas first.

Then photogrammetry plugs in at the end.
